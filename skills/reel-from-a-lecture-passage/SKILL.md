# Reel from a lecture passage

The map and the order of work. Read [PRINCIPLES.md](../../PRINCIPLES.md) first. The other skills cover each step in
depth; this one says what comes when, and what a good passage is.

## What a good reel is

- **One complete idea in the speaker's own words, 20 to 90 seconds.** An advice, a saying, a benefit drawn from a
  verse or a hadith, a lesson from the Sira, a diagnosis of a problem, a glad tiding, a question with its answer.
- It **opens where the thought opens and ends where it lands**, and it is understood rightly without what came
  before. If not, widen the cut or choose another passage. Never "explain" it with added text.
- **One voice.** No questioner's or host's voice, unless the clip is a question and its answer.
- It passes the sharia test standing alone (see `reel-sharia-guard`).

## Lecture cards

A *card* (or excerpt) is a short span of a lecture already picked out, classified (wassiya, saying, benefit from a
verse or a hadith, lesson, diagnosis, glad tiding, Q&A …) and anchored to its exact place in the transcript. Cards
are the best starting point: someone already judged that the span is complete and faithful. Treat them as a
shortlist, not a guarantee:

- Prefer cards from lectures whose extraction was audited.
- A card's summary or explanation is written for an index, not his words. Only its verbatim text is his.
- A card's start and end can stop inside a sentence. Widen to the sentence edges (see `reel-cutting-on-sentences`),
  never dropping any part of the card.
- Skip a card whose title would have to be cut to fit the screen, or rewrite the title as a short summary under the
  title rules (see `reel-verbatim-captions`).

When there is no card, a transcript stretch can serve, but it has not been reviewed as a unit: it needs more care
with context, speaker changes and the sharia guard.

## Order of work

Do these in order. Each step is cheaper than the next, so a problem found early costs least.

1. **Choose.** Search by subject (in Arabic). Read the candidate's text. Reject early: needs context, two voices,
   munkar presented neutrally, a ruling on one case that would carry over wrongly, a doubtful hearing.
2. **Read the context.** Read about a dozen transcript segments before and after. Does the idea start earlier? Does
   it land later? Is the speaker quoting someone he goes on to refute? Is there a question being answered?
3. **Settle the cut before fetching anything.** Whole sentences, 20 to 90 s, edges between words with a little air
   (`reel-cutting-on-sentences`). Write the first and last words down; check the cut against them later.
4. **Check the words.** Every segment in the cut should have measured word times. If not, find the true edges by ear
   and expect caption drift (`reel-verbatim-captions`). Mark every recited verse and hadith
   (`reel-quran-and-hadith`).
5. **Fetch only the section** you need, with a few seconds of margin, at up to 1080p.
6. **Frame** the 16:9 picture into 9:16 (`reel-framing-and-layout`).
7. **Caption** with his words, word-timed, from the corrected transcript.
8. **Design** title, captions, any frames for verses and hadith, motion, outro (`reel-motion-and-animation`).
9. **Render, then retime** (trim silences, mild speed), never inside a recitation.
10. **Check** mechanically, then by eye and ear (`reel-review-and-approval`).
11. **Write the post caption** with attribution: speaker, lecture title, timestamped link to the original.
12. **Hand it to the person responsible** for approval. Nothing is published before that.

## Keep a record per reel

Store, next to each finished reel: the source lecture and its link, the cut (seconds and first/last words), the
caption text, the look you chose, the retime you applied, and the results of each check. You will need them to
re-make the reel with one change only (`reel-edit-requests`) and to answer "why was this posted".

## Checklist

- [ ] One complete idea, 20–90 s, on whole sentences; context read before and after.
- [ ] One voice (or a question with its answer).
- [ ] Passes the sharia test standing alone.
- [ ] Every segment of the cut has measured word times, or the edges were found by ear.
- [ ] Verses and hadith marked and handled per `reel-quran-and-hadith`.
- [ ] Record kept: source, cut, caption, look, retime, checks.
- [ ] Approved by the person responsible before publishing.
