# The sharia guard

A reel is a standalone card. Whatever the lecture meant in its flow, the viewer sees only these seconds. Apply this
guard at selection, again after the cut is widened, and again on the finished reel.

## The test

> *Standing alone, does this reel counsel toward good, rule, warn or condemn?*

- **Keep** when it does.
- **Reject** when it presents a munkar (wine, fornication, obscenity, blasphemy, magic, usury, …) approvingly or
  neutrally, even when the words are his, verbatim.

## Passages

1. **A sin named to condemn it** may appear only when the condemnation is inside the clip, and the title leads with the
   lesson or the warning, never with the sin. Such passages come from excerpts whose own frame is a lesson: a lesson
   from the Sira, a diagnosis, a prohibition drawn from a verse or hadith, or an answer that refutes a doubt. An
   "advice", "saying" or "glad tiding" clip never carries one.
2. **Unreviewed transcript stretches carry no munkar term at all.** A keyword list is a tripwire, not a judge: it
   catches candidates for a person to read. Expect false alarms (the hijab verse's «بخمرهن» is not خمر) and add
   exemptions as you find them, never loosen the rule.
3. **Context.** Drop a passage that needs what came before to be read rightly, or whose ruling on one particular case
   would be wrongly carried over to another.
4. **Settled matters only.** "Against the sharia" means what the Quran, the Sunnah and scholarly agreement make plain.
   A matter scholars differ on is not framed as settled, and is not used to "answer" someone.
5. **No fatwa framing.** The reel and its caption give no ruling of their own. Any ruling is only his recorded words.
   If your source says it gives no fatwa, the clip must not look like one.
6. **No false claims.** Never say or imply that he commented on a current event he did not address. His recorded
   teaching may be set beside a matter of today, as his earlier words.
7. **No person is mocked or judged.** Answer ideas. No party politics, no call to disorder.
8. **Tragedy** is answered with patience and mercy, never exploited.

## Pictures

- **Only the lecture's own picture** of the speaker, unless outside footage was screened shot by shot
  (`reel-outside-footage`).
- **Never a woman, in any form**: on screen, in the background, on a poster, drawn or animated, small or far. **Not
  even blurred**: a blur still shows shape and colour. A shot with a woman gets no picture at all.
- Never nakedness, alcohol, drugs, gambling, dancing, a musical performance, violence as entertainment, idols or
  worship of other religions, fortune-telling or magic.
- **No gore.** News footage of war or disaster without blood, bodies or wounds is allowed.
- A shot that cannot be made clean is fully covered (opaque, over still things only) or left out. Never a soft blur.
- Any drawn or generated imagery pictures no person.

## Sound

- **No music** and no sound bed under his voice. If an outside clip carries music, remove it (a voice-isolation model)
  or use the clip without its sound.

## Captions and titles

- The title leads with the lesson, never with the sin.
- No model-written ruling words (حرام، يجوز، كفر، بدعة، مخالف للشرع) in any line you wrote. Watch for false hits on sacred
  names (المسجد الحرام).
- News and awareness captions never say «ترند», «حديث الناس», «يتداول», «الكل يتحدث» and never speak of «أجندة»,
  «مخطط», «مؤامرة» or hidden intentions. Awareness describes what the source documents.

## Checklist

- [ ] Standing alone, the reel counsels, rules, warns or condemns.
- [ ] Any sin named is condemned inside the clip; the title leads with the lesson.
- [ ] No context needed; no ruling that carries over wrongly; no disputed matter framed as settled.
- [ ] No fatwa framing; no claim he commented on today's event.
- [ ] Pictures: only his lecture's picture, or screened footage; no woman, not blurred, no gore.
- [ ] No music.
- [ ] A person read the passage and watched the reel.
