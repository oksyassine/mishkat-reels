# Picture and motion

Items marked **taste** are choices that worked for us, not rules.

## Fill the frame unless the face leaves the crop

**Recommendation.** Crop the 16:9 lecture to a full 9:16 frame of the speaker whenever his face stays inside the crop
in every frame of the cut. Keep the whole picture only when it does not (two people, a wide moving shot, his face lost
for seconds).

**Why.** On a phone, a filled frame is a person speaking to you; a thin strip of a wide hall is a video of a video. Our
filled reels were the ones that got watched.

**Lesson.** The first rule only filled perfectly centred, still shots, which left most reels as a small strip. It was
relaxed step by step: a little slack around the face, then filling even when the camera moves as long as the face stays
in the crop.

## Let the crop follow his face

**Recommendation.** When a single still window cannot hold him, move the window with his face: still while his face
is comfortably inside, a slow ease when he moves, a jump on a camera cut, never off his face.

**Lesson.** A still window cut his face at the edge when he leaned. Tracking the eyes, nose and mouth (not the centre
of the face box) fixed it.

## Never a blurred copy behind a moving camera

**Recommendation.** When the whole picture is kept and the camera moves, use a plain, still or gradient background,
never a blurred moving copy of the same shot.

**Why.** A blurred background that pans and shakes with the camera is distracting and looks like a mistake.

## Crop to where the head moves

**Recommendation.** If the speaker sways within a band, crop the sides to that band (plus a margin) instead of keeping
the full width, so the picture is taller.

**Lesson.** A seated speaker who sways came out as a thin band of the whole wide picture until the crop followed where
his head moved.

## Nothing over his face

**Recommendation.** Measure his face and keep title, captions, key lines, callouts and badges off it.

**Lesson.** A batch of reels was held for text on a forehead, a number between the eyes, and a key line across the
face. The fix was in the renderer (placing everything around a measured face band), not in each reel.

## Natural colours

**Recommendation.** No colour grading, grain, light leaks or film filters on the speaker's picture.

**Lesson.** A "vintage" look turned a reel sepia; grain also multiplied file size several times. All grading was
removed.

## Legible captions over any clothing

**Recommendation.** A soft dark band under caption lines whenever the picture reaches them.

**Lesson.** White words vanished on a white robe.

## Covers: the best frame, not any frame

**Recommendation.** Score candidate frames (face found, size, nearness to the middle, sharpness), then pick one with
eyes open and a calm expression. Put any title low, never on the face.

## Calm motion by default (taste)

**Recommendation.** A calm family of motion by default: no glitch, shake, whip or crash zoom. Within it, keep something
gently moving (captions, a title sweep, a slow push of about 6 %).

**Lesson.** A reel that came across as glitchy and distorted was traced to a strong zoom push-pull; calm became the default.
Later, a little more steady motion was asked for, inside the calm family.

## A see-through title, briefly (taste)

**Recommendation.** The title on a see-through card for the first ~10 seconds, sized down until it fits whole, then
gone.

**Lesson.** A title once hid his face, and others ran out of their box because they were measured in one font and
drawn in another.

## One look per reel, varied (taste)

**Recommendation.** Keep a library of looks, draw one per reel (seeded so a re-render reproduces it), and avoid
repeating the previous reel's choices.
