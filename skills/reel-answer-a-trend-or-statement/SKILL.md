# Answering a trend, a news item or a public statement

The reel is never a news report and never the scholar "commenting on" today's event. It is his earlier teaching set
beside what people face today, and it says so honestly.

## Hard rules

1. **Agree the passage before rendering.** Show the person responsible up to three candidate passages as text (title,
   lecture, why it answers, the verbatim words, the seconds). Render only the one they agree to. A time-out proposes;
   it never renders.
2. **No automatic trend reels and no automatic posts.** Nothing is rendered or published by a schedule that nobody
   asked for.
3. **Never claim he answered the event.** No «يرد الشيخ على…», «علّق على…», «جواب الشيخ على…» in any form.
4. **No verdict from the writer of the caption.** The ruling is only his recorded words.
5. **Settled matters only** (see `reel-sharia-guard`). A disputed matter is offered as a general subject or left out.
6. **Answer the idea, never the person.** Never name, mock or judge anyone; no party politics.
7. **Verify what you state.** A news or awareness subject needs a source you checked; a statement's quote must be found
   in its source (below). A false alarm is a lie told in the account's name.
8. **Honest zero.** If no passage truly answers, say so. Do not substitute a general virtue on a neighbouring matter.

## What comes first (our order; adapt it to your audience)

1. **A decision:** a state's law, ban or policy that goes against the sharia or strikes at Muslims and their rites,
   ranked by how many Muslims it touches.
2. **A statement:** a known public figure's تصريح against the sharia, or a wrong idea about the religion, the family,
   money or what a life is for.
3. **A concept:** a viral idea, campaign or habit carrying something against the sharia. The answer is correcting the
   concept (تصحيح المفاهيم) and diagnosing reality (تشخيص الواقع).
4. **General:** other trends, seasons, grief, anxiety. A tragedy is answered with patience and mercy, never exploited.

Three purposes need different captions:

| purpose | what it is | caption |
|---|---|---|
| trend | what people are discussing now | may name what is discussed |
| news | a confirmed event touching many Muslims, need not be trending | names what happened and to whom; never "people are talking" |
| awareness | a documented public measure, campaign, product or idea | calm, describes what the source documents; no «أجندة / مخطط / مؤامرة», no hidden intentions |

"New trends" means new subjects: keep a record of subjects already offered, and compare by title, not by shared
keywords (two different subjects can share «تربية الأبناء»).

## Searching the lectures

- **The matter itself first.** Search first for the matter by name (فرضية الحجاب), then for the situation as a Muslim
  meets it, then for the wider principle. Give each query its share of results in turn, so general queries do not
  crowd out the direct ones.
- **Keywords as he would say them** (الربا، قوم لوط، نزع الحجاب), not news words, names or countries. Search the
  transcripts letter for letter as well as by meaning; weight rare words more.
- **Three shapes of answer:** his teaching of the ruling on the matter (بيان الحق); a lesson from those before (عبرة);
  his diagnosis and correction (تشخيص وتصحيح).
- **Rank:** the matter itself by name outranks a general virtue; best of all, a case he describes that mirrors the
  event. Drop: a shared word only; a passage needing context; a different particular case whose ruling would carry
  over wrongly; anything that would mock.
- Excerpts already reviewed are safer than raw transcript stretches; a stretch carries no munkar term at all, one voice
  only, no doubtful hearing, 20–90 s.

## Statements (تصريح ثم الجواب)

1. **The quote is the source's own words.** Copy it exactly as the report prints it (at least about six words), never
   paraphrased or self-translated. Fetch the source and check the quote is in it, comparing letters only (diacritics,
   hamza forms, ى/ي, ة/ه folded). Not found: drop the option, or keep the subject but print no quote.
2. **Claim by claim.** List what was actually said, claim by claim, with keywords for each and the evidence a scholar
   would cite against it. A passage answering every claim outranks one answering a single claim. A general virtue is
   not an answer.
3. **Opening with the statement's own clip** (optional): search for the speaker's video, locate the quote in its
   subtitles by text (a sliding window, a high match ratio, at most about 45 s), and set the edges so no neighbouring
   word slips in. One extra second once let in the first word of an accusation the speaker went on to make.
4. **A woman speaker is never shown**, not even blurred: her voice over a plain card with a sound wave, her words as
   captions from the video's own subtitles. Any other speaker's clip is screened shot by shot (`reel-outside-footage`).
5. **No name and no source on screen.** A neutral label such as «تصريحٌ متداول» only. The caption quotes the words, with
   no speaker line and no outlet. The person approving gets the link and seconds privately.
6. **Any failure leaves the reply alone.** If no clip is found or it fails screening, the reel is the reply, the caption
   still quotes the checked words, and the person approving is told why.
7. Bring the clip's loudness to the reply's (reported speech in reaction videos is often far quieter).

## Captions

- Order that worked: (the checked statement, words only) → one hook sentence that names the idea as the question
  people ask → one bridge sentence presenting the clip as guidance from an earlier lecture → his words, verbatim, cut at
  a word → speaker and lecture name → keywords → a few hashtags (we keep up to five).
- No link, handle or phone number except your own site; no long «…» quote except his verbatim words.
- Run a caption check for the forbidden phrases above, but treat it as a tripwire, not a proof. The person reading it
  before approval is the real check.

## Series

One subject can run over a few days, each day a different lecture. Keep it short: we cap a series at three reels,
because reach fell sharply after the first.

## Checklist

- [ ] The subject is verified; the statement's quote was found in its source.
- [ ] The passage answers the matter itself (claim by claim for a statement), and was agreed before rendering.
- [ ] No claim he commented on the event; no verdict of the caption writer; no person named, mocked or judged.
- [ ] News and awareness captions free of "people are talking" and plot words.
- [ ] Statement clip: no woman shown, no name or source on screen, edges clean, loudness matched.
- [ ] Reviewed and approved like any reel (`reel-review-and-approval`).
