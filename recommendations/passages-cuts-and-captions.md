# Passages, cuts and captions

## The passage is agreed before rendering

**Recommendation.** Show the person responsible the candidate passages as text (title, lecture, the verbatim words,
the seconds, why it fits) and render only the one they agree to.

**Why.** Reading a passage takes a minute; a render and its review take far longer. A wrong passage found after the
render costs the whole cycle, and an unasked render may end up published by mistake.

**Lesson.** Reels were first rendered automatically after a time-out if no choice was made. The person responsible
asked to see the chosen part of the lecture first, every time. The time-out now only proposes.

## Answer the matter itself first

**Recommendation.** When a reel answers a subject, prefer a passage that names the matter itself over one about a
general virtue nearby. Search first for the matter by name, then for the situation as people meet it, then for the
wider principle.

**Why.** A general virtue on a neighbouring matter looks like an evasion, and viewers notice.

**Lesson.** For a ban on a religious practice, the first answer chosen was about steadfastness in hard times. The
scholar's direct words on the practice itself existed but were invisible, because broad queries had crowded out the
direct ones. Search order and ranking were fixed so the direct answer comes first.

## A statement is answered claim by claim

**Recommendation.** When answering a public statement, list what was actually said, claim by claim, and pick the
passage that answers those claims.

**Why.** A reply that answers something else is not a reply.

## Cut on whole sentences, settled before anything is fetched

**Recommendation.** Fix the first and last words of the cut on sentence edges before downloading or rendering, and
place the edges in the word timings.

**Why.** A reel that ends on a sentence only started changes what he said, and it is invisible on a still frame.

**Lesson.** For a while, the cut was only checked after the render, and only reported. Several published reels ended on
a sentence just begun. The cut is now settled first.

## Captions come from the corrected transcript, not the auto-subtitles

**Recommendation.** Build captions from the transcript you have corrected (restored verses, fixed names, removed
hallucinations), timed with word timings, never directly from the platform's automatic subtitle file.

**Why.** Auto-subtitles are raw speech recognition. If captions read the subtitle file, no correction ever reaches a reel.

**Lesson.** Early reels burned raw recogniser text even after the transcript had been repaired, because the subtitle
file was read first.

## Measured word times, not estimates

**Recommendation.** Use word timings measured against the audio (forced alignment, or the platform's own per-word
tags matched to your transcript by text). If a lecture has none, get them before cutting a reel from it.

**Why.** Rolling auto-caption windows lag the speech by up to a line; times estimated from them put captions seconds
late.

**Lesson.** The reels of a whole playlist had late captions until word tags were imported for its lectures.

## Silences are trimmed, never words

**Recommendation.** Shorten a pause only where the word gap is long **and** the audio is quiet. Never cut on word gaps
alone. Never cut or speed a recitation.

**Why.** Aligners sometimes squeeze words together and leave a false gap over speech.

**Lesson.** A phrase vanished from the middle of a reply, and later ten seconds of speech were cut from another reel as
"silence", because the cutter trusted word gaps. Cuts now require quiet sound as well.

## A mild speed-up, never on the Quran

**Recommendation.** Normal speed or about 1.1x; keep recitation at 1.0x; keep the speed an old reel was made at when
re-making it.

## Show a phrase twice only if he said it twice

**Recommendation.** Remove rolling-caption repeats at segment joins as a reviewed repair, checked against the subtitle
file's own word stream.

**Lesson.** A reel already approved showed two phrases twice. Approval was withdrawn, the repeats were removed over the
spans in use, and a sync check now runs before any review.

## Spell clearly, quote nothing that is not his

**Recommendation.** On screen, restore the hamza or madda a transcript left out of a word whose reading is not in
doubt (القرآن, not «القران»), as part of diacritic polish; leave ambiguous forms alone; and never let a title look
like his quote (no quotation marks, no attribution, no ruling).
Lecture names on screen carry no emoji, which render as empty squares.
