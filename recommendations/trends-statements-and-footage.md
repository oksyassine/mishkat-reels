# Trends, statements and outside footage

## What goes against the sharia comes first

**Recommendation (our order).** When choosing which current matter to answer, put first a state decision against the
sharia or against Muslims (ranked by how many Muslims it touches), then a public statement carrying a wrong idea, then
a viral concept to correct, then general subjects.

**Why.** A channel of scholars' words has a responsibility toward the people who watch it; the measure is how many
Muslims a matter touches and how deeply.

## Settled matters only

**Recommendation.** "Against the sharia" means what the Quran, the Sunnah and scholarly agreement make plain. A matter
scholars differ on is offered as a general subject, never as something to "answer".

## News is not "people are talking"; awareness is not a plot

**Recommendation.** A news caption names what happened and to whom. An awareness caption describes what the source
documents, calmly. Neither claims that something is trending, nor speaks of agendas, schemes or conspiracies.

**Why.** A false alarm is a lie told in the account's name.

## Quote a statement only from its source

**Recommendation.** Copy a public figure's words exactly as the report prints them and check they are in it before
using them. If they are not found, print no quote.

**Lesson.** Reports often paraphrase. One statement had to be checked against the speaker's own video subtitles because
the reports did not carry the exact words.

## No name and no source on screen

**Recommendation.** An opening statement or clip carries only a neutral label («تصريحٌ متداول», «مقطعٌ متداول»). The
caption quotes the words only, with no speaker line and no outlet.

**Why.** The reel answers an idea, not a person, and is not meant to promote the speaker or their page.

## No woman shown, not even blurred

**Recommendation.** A shot with a woman anywhere in it gets no picture at all: her voice (if she is the speaker) over a
plain card with a sound wave. Never a blur, never a box over her.

**Lesson.** A blurred version was tried first; it still showed shape and colour, and was refused. A box over a woman was
also allowed briefly; a box can miss a hand or hair as she moves, so it was replaced by no picture.

## Edges never take in a neighbouring word

**Recommendation.** Set a clip's edges between words, never padded into the next word.

**Lesson.** One extra second at the end of a statement let in the first word of an accusation the speaker went on to
make, which changed what the clip seemed to say.

## Match the loudness

**Recommendation.** Bring an opening clip to the reply's loudness.

**Lesson.** A clip taken from a reaction video opened far quieter than the reply, and the reel seemed silent at first.

## Footage without speech is usable

**Recommendation.** A short video with no speech (CCTV, a fire, a crowd) can open a reel as it is. Do not assume it
needs a transcript; and say "no subtitles" rather than "no speech" when you have not listened.

## News footage without gore is allowed

**Recommendation.** Ruins, soldiers, vehicles and smoke may be shown; blood, bodies and wounds may not.

**Lesson.** A clean clip went to voice only because a ruins backdrop alarmed the screener; the screening rules now say
plainly what is fine.

## Short series

**Recommendation.** Keep a series on one subject to about three reels, each from a different lecture.

**Lesson.** Reach fell sharply after the first reply in a long series on one subject.

## "New" means a new subject

**Recommendation.** When asked for new subjects, filter out what was already offered, comparing titles rather than
shared keywords.

**Lesson.** The same two subjects came back in consecutive "new" lists, and an unrelated subject was wrongly dropped
because it shared a keyword with an old one.
