# Principles

Every reel puts a scholar's words in front of strangers, cut out of the lecture's flow, often watched without
sound and shared without context. That is a trust (أمانة) toward the speaker, toward the viewer and toward the
religion itself. These principles come before quality, speed, reach and cost. A change that breaks one of them is
wrong even when the reel looks better.

Everything else in this repository (the skills, the recommendations, the taste notes) is a way of keeping these.

## 1. His words, verbatim

- The words on screen are the speaker's own words, as he said them, in his order. Captions come from a transcript
  that was checked against the audio, and each word is timed to the moment he says it.
- The only change allowed to his words on screen is light polish: diacritics and punctuation. Nothing is added,
  dropped, reordered or paraphrased inside a sentence.
- If what you hear differs from the transcript, fix the transcript as a reviewed repair with a stated source (the
  audio you heard), or choose another passage. Never "correct" him on screen.

## 2. No machine-written words presented as his

- A model may help you find, rank or check a passage. It never writes words that appear as the speaker's.
- A short title may summarise the clip. It is then yours, not his: never in quotation marks, never attributed to
  him, never carrying a ruling (حرام، يجوز، بدعة …), and never cut off on screen.
- A post caption that quotes him quotes one unbroken run of his words from the clip.
- No translation, summary or "explanation" of his words is shown as if he said it. If a clip needs added text to be
  understood rightly, widen the cut or choose another passage.

## 3. The Quran is never wrong

- A recited verse is shown in the mushaf text, exactly, never in transcript text and never retyped.
- Show only what he recited. If he recited part of a verse, show that part with its reference and do not present it
  as the whole verse. If you cannot identify the verse with certainty, show no verse text: his recitation stays as
  sound.
- **Riwaya.** Use the mushaf text of the riwaya he recites in. Never lay Hafs text over a Warsh recitation (or the
  reverse). If you do not have the right text, show none.
- A recitation plays at its own speed and is never cut inside. No playful, glitching or shaking effect on a verse.
- No automatic bulk change to verse text. Each fix is heard and reviewed by a person.

## 4. Hadith with care

- A hadith is shown as he said it, in his wording. Do not label it «قال النبي ﷺ» on your own (he may be quoting a
  narrator or paraphrasing), and add no grade he did not state.

## 5. Honest zero

- If no passage truly answers the subject, say so. Never stretch a passage on a neighbouring matter, pad a series,
  or force a clip to meet a quota or a schedule.
- If a check could not run, the reel has not passed it. "No answer" from a reviewer, a tool or a person is never a
  pass.

## 6. The sharia guard

Each reel stands alone, out of the lecture's flow. The test, for every reel:

> *Standing alone, does this reel counsel toward good, rule, warn or condemn?*
> If it presents a munkar (wine, fornication, obscenity, blasphemy, magic, usury, …) approvingly or neutrally,
> it is not made.

- A sin he names in order to condemn it may appear only when the condemnation is inside the clip and the title
  leads with the lesson or the warning, never with the sin.
- Drop a passage that needs its context to be read rightly, or whose ruling on one particular case would be wrongly
  carried over to another.
- Matters scholars differ on are not presented as settled. The reel gives no ruling of its own: any ruling is only
  his recorded words.
- **Pictures.** Only the lecture's own picture, unless outside footage has been screened shot by shot. Never show a
  woman, in any form, not even blurred; nor nakedness, alcohol, gambling, dancing, a musical performance, idols or
  gore. A shot that cannot be made clean is fully covered or left out, never softly blurred.
- **Sound.** No music and no sound bed under his voice.

## 7. Truthful framing

- Never say or imply that he commented on a current event he did not address. His recorded teaching may be set
  beside a matter of today, as his earlier words.
- Answer an idea, never a person. No mocking, no judging, no party politics.
- A news or awareness caption states what happened and to whom. It never claims that "people are talking about it",
  and never speaks of plots or hidden intentions.
- A quoted statement by anyone else is checked against its source before it is used.

## 8. Attribution

Every reel names the speaker as he is publicly known, the lecture's title, and a link to the exact moment in the
original lecture, so anyone can hear the words in full context. The source is the lecture as published by its own
channel; a clip from another channel credits that speaker and points to that channel.

## 9. A person decides what is published

- Every reel is watched and approved by the person responsible before it is published, scheduled reels included.
- An approval covers the version that was watched. A re-made or edited reel is a new reel and needs a new approval;
  a replaced version can no longer be published.
- No reel is made, and nothing is published, by an automatic process that nobody asked for.
- Judgements on his words, on the Quran, on hadith and on rulings are reviewed by a person. Tools can flag; they
  do not absolve.
